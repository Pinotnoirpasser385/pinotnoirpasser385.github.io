📺 aceHub - Stream AceStream to Any Device Instantly

aceHub is a small, fast helper for your home network. It takes one AceStream link and shares it with every device in your house — your TV, your tablet, your phone — at the same time. It runs quietly in the background, needs no tinkering, and is built for Android TV.

What Is aceHub and Why Would You Want It?

If you have ever tried to watch a stream on more than one screen at home, you probably know the problem: the stream plays on one device, but the second device has to fetch the same content all over again. That eats your internet connection, causes buffering, and makes everyone in the room unhappy.

aceHub fixes that. It is a headless hub, which simply means it works in the background without a big fancy window on your screen. You start it, it sits quietly on your computer, and it acts like a traffic manager for streams. One device fetches the content, and aceHub passes it along to all the other devices on your home network — your LAN — so nobody has to download the same thing twice.

It works on port 8000, it uses 0 transcode (meaning the video is passed through exactly as it arrives, with no quality loss and no heavy lifting), and it responds in under 50 milliseconds (TTFB, or time to first byte). In plain language: the picture starts almost instantly, and your computer barely breaks a sweat.

✨ Key Features at a Glance

  • One stream, many screens. Share a single stream with every TV, phone, tablet, and computer on your home network at the same time.
  • Headless and out of the way. aceHub runs in the background. No cluttered window, no distractions. Start it and forget it.
  • No quality loss. With zero transcoding, the video you see is exactly the video that arrived. Nothing is re-compressed or softened.
  • Very low latency. A time to first byte of under 50ms means streams begin playing almost the moment you press play.
  • Lightweight and fast. Built for performance, so it runs happily on an everyday Windows computer without eating all your memory.
  • Made for Android TV. Point your Android TV player at your computer's address and port 8000, and you are watching in seconds.
  • Universal LAN proxy. It does not care which app or box you use. If it can open a network stream, it can work with aceHub.

📋 What You Need Before You Start

Nothing exotic. If you have an ordinary home computer and a TV, you almost certainly have everything on this list already.

  • A Windows computer running Windows 10 or Windows 11 (64-bit recommended). This is the machine that will run aceHub.
  • A home internet connection — a normal broadband connection is fine. A wired connection to your router gives the smoothest results, but Wi-Fi works too.
  • A home network where your computer and your TV are connected to the same router. This is the "LAN" part, and it is what makes the sharing possible.
  • An Android TV device — a TV with Android TV built in, or an Android TV box or streaming stick connected to your television.
  • A stream player app on the TV that can open a network address. Most people already have one installed.
  • About ten minutes of your time, most of which is spent waiting for the download to finish.

🎬 Using aceHub for the First Time

Once aceHub is running, it listens quietly on port 8000. Think of port 8000 as the door number of your computer on the home network — it tells other devices exactly where to knock.

To use it, you need to know your computer's local address. Here is how to find it, without any technical knowledge:

  1. On your Windows computer, click the Start button and type cmd, then press Enter. A small black window opens.
  2. Type ipconfig and press Enter.
  3. Look for the line that says IPv4 Address. It will look something like 192.168.1.25. Write that number down.
  4. Your aceHub address is that number followed by :8000. For example: http://192.168.1.25:8000.
  5. Close the black window. You will not need it again.

Keep that address somewhere handy. You will type it into your TV once, and then you can save it as a favourite so you never have to type it again.

📡 Playing Streams on Your Android TV

  1. Make sure your Android TV and your Windows computer are connected to the same home router. This is the most common reason things do not work.
  2. Open the stream player app on your Android TV.
  3. Choose the option to open a network stream or a custom address. Wording varies between apps, but it is usually called something like Open URL, Add stream, or Network stream.
  4. Type the aceHub address you wrote down, including the port number. For example: http://192.168.1.25:8000.
  5. Confirm, and the stream should begin playing within a second or two. Thanks to the sub-50ms response time, there is almost no waiting.
  6. Open the same address on a second device, and it will play there too — without pulling the same content down from the internet again.

Because aceHub does not transcode, the video arrives on your TV exactly as it left the source. That means no waiting for the computer to convert anything, no dropped frames from an overloaded processor, and no drop in picture quality.

🔧 Troubleshooting Common Issues

The stream does not start on my TV

  • Check that aceHub is still running on your computer. If you closed it, start it again.
  • Check that both devices are on the same home network. A TV on a separate guest network will not be able to reach your computer.
  • Re-check the address. A missing :8000 at the end is the single most common mistake.

The picture stutters

  • If your computer uses Wi-Fi, try connecting it to the router with a cable. This alone fixes most stutter.
  • Move closer to the router if you are on Wi-Fi, or reduce the number of devices streaming at the same time.
  • Close other programs on the computer that are using a lot of internet bandwidth.

Windows shows a warning when I open the app

  • Click More info, then Run anyway. Windows shows this for many independent applications that have not paid for a commercial signature.

I cannot find the file I downloaded

  • Open File Explorer and click Downloads on the left side. Sort the folder by date, and your file will be at the top.
  • In most browsers you can also press Ctrl + J to see your download list.
